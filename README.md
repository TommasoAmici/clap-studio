# Clap Studio

A macOS (SwiftUI + Metal) app to organise and search audio samples for music production.
Point it at a folder; it indexes the files **in place** (nothing is moved, renamed or changed).

- **Similarity search**: select a sample and see the closest-sounding ones (a hi-hat finds other hi-hats).
- **Explore map**: every sample as a dot, laid out by similarity and coloured by cluster. Clusters are named after what is in them (for example "Snare · Bright" or "Hi-hat & Cymbal"); hover a legend row for the breakdown. Hover a dot to hear it, click to select, drag it out into Ableton. Pan with a drag or trackpad scroll, zoom with pinch or mouse wheel, double-click empty space to refit.
- **Shuffle** (⌘R, Library tab): puts the list in a random order so browsing doesn't always start with the same samples. Press again for another order; sorting by a column, or ✕ next to the button, goes back. (Rescan Library is ⇧⌘R.)
- **Preview**: select a row (or press space) to hear it; clicking the waveform plays from that point.
- **Drag and drop** any sample (table row, map dot, the inspector waveform, similar-sounds list) into Ableton or any DAW. Each drag is counted; the "Uses" column and the *Most used* / *Never used* / *Recently grabbed* filters show which samples you keep reusing.
- **Cue points**: read from WAV/AIFF files, edit in the app (click the waveform to audition from a spot, `M` sets a cue exactly there, option-click, drag flags, 1-9 to jump, undo/redo). Edits are saved automatically: the app's database is updated at once and, a moment later, WAV files get the cues embedded (only the cue chunks change; the write is verified and atomic) so other software sees them. Other formats keep their cues in the app only.
- **Sound properties**: loudness, brightness, tempo (loops), pitch or key, and loop vs one-shot are measured at indexing time. They show as sortable columns and filter from the bar above the list and map; the tempo can be corrected in the inspector.
- **Categories**: with CLAP, every sample is sorted into one of 39 categories by comparing it to text descriptions: musical ones (kick, snare, rimshot, cowbell, pad, vocal, ...), sounds of the world outside (birds, water, rain, wind, speech, traffic, machinery, ...) and *Field recording* for long files. They appear in the sidebar and can be overridden per sample.
- **Moods**: nine moods (Aggressive, Rebellious, Powerful, Epic, Dark, Dreamy, Happy, Uplifting, Groovy) are scored with CLAP the same way, but a sound can have several, and each mood is judged against your own library (a sound gets a mood when it stands out for it). They appear in the sidebar, a Mood column and the filter bar, where moods can be combined. They are recomputed when the library changes; most one-shots have no mood. Set your own moods in the properties (tick any number) or for a whole selection from the right-click menu (*Set Mood*: add, remove, clear); they replace the automatic ones, survive recomputing, are undoable, and *Use automatic* brings the automatic ones back.
- **Field recordings**: files of 30 seconds or more are treated as field recordings, and so is any file you put in the Field recording category by hand, however short (it is read again in segments; take the category away and it is read as a single sound again). They are read as a stream (however long), cut at silences (and at 10 s in steady sound), and each segment is embedded on its own after its silent edges are trimmed; the file's own embedding is the length-weighted average of its segments, so the map, clusters and *Find Similar* work as for any sample. They get the *Field recording* category, a *Field recordings* entry in the type filter above the list (one-shots and loops leave them out) and their own map colour. Moods come from the segments, judged against your ordinary samples, and a recording lists every mood any segment has. Searching by description, dropping a file on the window and *Find Similar* score a recording by its best segment and show where it is (an *at 2:05* tag in the list); selecting the result starts playing there. Properties show the number of segments and a *Sounds in this recording* list (birds, water, speech, ...: each segment is named with the same text descriptions as the categories, up to two sounds per segment) with the total time of each and timestamps that start playback there; clicking a sound's name filters the list by it. The sidebar and the Category filter count a recording under each sound heard in it, so *Birds* shows ordinary bird samples and recordings with birds. Tempo, key and pitch are not measured. Existing long files are re-indexed once after updating.
- **Bird species** (optional): with [BirdNET](https://github.com/birdnet-team/BirdNET-Analyzer) installed, field recordings are listened to for birds after indexing (3 s at a time, about 200x faster than real time). *Birds in this recording* in the properties lists each species heard with at least 80% confidence, with its timestamps (click one to play from there) and the confidence of its clearest moment. Tell the app where a recording was made (pick it on a map in its properties, or set a default under Settings, Birds) and species that do not live there are ruled out, using BirdNET's location model; changing the place filters at once and listens to the recording again so the place applies from the start. Create the model files once with `mise exec uv@latest -- uv run --python 3.12 --default-index https://pypi.org/simple tools/export_birdnet.py` (downloads about 220 MB; it checks the Core ML output against the original). `scripts/bundle.sh` and the release workflow bundle the model files when they exist (the workflow exports them), so a built app needs no setup. BirdNET's models are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: a build with the feature is for non-commercial use only, and its notice is included in the bundle (`licenses/BirdNET.txt`). The feature is compiled in by default, and every local and release build includes it for now; build with `NON_COMMERCIAL=0` (`NON_COMMERCIAL=0 scripts/bundle.sh`, `NON_COMMERCIAL=0 swift build`, or change `NON_COMMERCIAL` in `.github/workflows/release.yml` for the release workflow) to leave out the code, the models and the notice, for a build you could sell.
- **Duplicates**: the sidebar's duplicates button finds identical files and near-identical sounds, keeps the best copy and hides the rest (reversible from the *Hidden* filter; files are never deleted).
- **Favorites and collections**: star samples (`F`) and group them into collections. *Copy To…* (library or map selection) copies several samples to a folder.
- **Sweep**: with a sample selected, `←` and `→` step through its closest sounds, previewing each. Drop any audio file on the window to find library sounds like it.
- **Search by humming**: the microphone button in the bar above the list and map listens (up to 10 seconds, ending by itself) and ranks the library by similarity to what it heard (the same search as dropping a file on the window, including field recordings and the similarity aspects). Beatbox a kick, tap a desk, hum a bass line. The recording is only used for the search and deleted; nothing is stored or sent. It needs the app bundle (`scripts/bundle.sh` adds the microphone permission text) and the microphone permission the first time.
- **Ideas**: the lightbulb lists never-used sounds that sit next to the samples you grab most.
- **Project tempo and key**: set them in the toolbar; loops then preview time-stretched to the tempo and tonal sounds transposed to the key (previews only; dragged files are untouched).
- **Map colour, axes and lasso**: colour dots by category, loop/one-shot, usage, tempo, loudness or brightness; lay the map out by any two measured properties instead of similarity; shift-drag a lasso to send the picked dots to a collection, favorites, a tag or Hidden.
- **Similarity aspects**: the sliders under *What counts as similar* weigh overall sound against brightness, pitch (same note), level/punch and length; the list, map highlight and `←` `→` sweep follow.
- **Kit builder**: *Build Kit* suggests a snare, clap, hats and more around a chosen kick, by character, level and pack, with alternatives per slot.
- **Drum machine**: a 16-step, 8-track sequencer (toolbar grid button) for trying sounds together at the project tempo. *Try in Drum Machine* in the kit builder loads each pick with a starter rhythm for its category, and ◀ ▶ swaps in the alternatives. *Add to Drum Machine* is in the library context menu. Drag a sample over the drum machine and a line shows where it will be inserted (the middle of a track swaps its sound instead); anywhere below the tracks, even in the status bar, adds it at the end. Mute, solo, volume and swing per pattern; tempo changes apply live. Each track has a dice (try a similar sound: same category and one-shot/loop, close to what is there, never one the kit already uses), a back arrow (the sound it had before, up to 20 swaps) and a lock; *Shuffle Kit* (dice in the header) swaps every unlocked track and plays the result, and the menu next to it sets how far a swap reaches (Close, Medium, Wide), following the *What counts as similar* sliders. The groove menu offers presets (House, Techno, Drum & Bass, Hip-Hop, Trap, Breakbeat, Reggaeton, Electro): picking one sets the project tempo and swing and gives each sound a rhythm that fits its category. *Save Pattern as Groove…* keeps your own pattern's rhythms, swing and the project tempo as a groove of your own. The choice is remembered between launches and also shapes sounds you add later. The pattern itself (sounds, steps, mixer, swing) and whether the drawer was open are restored on relaunch; sounds that left the library are dropped.
- **Trim**: set where a sample starts and ends (inspector, or drag the green edges on the waveform) with a short or longer fade-out. The file is never changed: the trim is stored in the index and used for previews, the drum machine and exports. *Export Trimmed Copy…* (or *Copy To…* with a format chosen) writes the trimmed part as WAV, optionally as 16/24-bit, mono and 44.1/48 kHz for samplers. Waveforms and trimming cover the whole file, however long.
- **Squid Salmple** (automatic, no setting): any connected USB drive with an `ALM022` folder is taken as one for the module, and the Squid features appear while it is plugged in and go away with it. Such drives appear in the sidebar with the sets, banks and Pool of the [ALM022 module](https://busycircuits.com/pages/alm022). Banks show their eight channels to hear, drag out or try in the drum machine. *Send to Squid…* (library menu, drum machine, library menu, or the drive) writes up to eight samples as a bank (`Bank N/1…8/Name.wav`, `info.txt`, optionally in a `Set NAME` folder) or any number into the Pool, converted to 16-bit 44.1 kHz mono, cut at 11 s and brought to the same loudness as the app's level matching (peaks kept below -1 dB; the sheet's *Match levels* switch turns it off), so samples play at similar volumes. Right-click a drive and choose *Normalize All Samples…* to bring everything already on it to that loudness (after a confirmation): only the audio inside each 16-bit WAV is rewritten, in place, and the category tag and the rest of the file are kept. Samples can also be added to the free channels of a bank that is already on the stick (the number on a bank is how many are free). The sets and banks are managed like folders in Finder: right-click to rename (in place), duplicate, move to another set, reveal or move to the Trash; make new sets and banks; take a single sample out of a channel. Names are never changed for you: what you type, and what is on the stick, is kept exactly, and an orange warning explains when the module may not read it as written (set names over 8 characters, bank names over 8 characters or with non-ASCII ones, which it shows cut). Names a folder cannot hold (`/ : * ? " < > |`) are refused. Folder names are matched without regard to case, as on a FAT32 stick. Each file sent keeps its category: it is written into the WAV as a standard `LIST`/`INFO` genre entry after the audio, and when the app reads the stick it uses that category (instead of its own guess), so the drum machine finds the right groove parts for a bank. Files without the tag are categorised automatically as before. Existing banks are never overwritten silently. Samples on a stick are analysed but kept out of the library, the map and the saved index, and are never written to.
- **Tags and export names**: free-text tags (searchable, in the sidebar); *Copy To…* names files from a template such as `{category} - {name} - {bpm}`.
- **Everyday**: multi-select with batch actions, undo for hide/favorite/tag/collection edits (option-command-Z), folders are watched and rescanned automatically, and previews can be level-matched.
- **Training data export** (Settings, Developer): *Export Embeddings…* writes a new folder with `embeddings.npy` (float32, one 512-number row per sample), `labels.csv` (path, category, whether it is a manual or automatic label, CLAP's own category and confidence, file fingerprint) and `metadata.json`. Samples whose category you set by hand export with it; *Manual labels only* leaves out the automatic ones. Field recordings and samples without a category are not exported, and the library is not changed. Load it with:

  ```python
  import numpy as np, pandas as pd
  X = np.load("embeddings.npy")            # (N, 512), row i belongs to labels.csv row i
  labels = pd.read_csv("labels.csv")       # train on labels["category"]; group by "fingerprint" to split duplicates apart
  ```

## Persistence

All expensive results are stored in `~/Library/Application Support/ClapStudio/library.sqlite` (override with `CLAP_DATA_DIR`): file index, embeddings (and the segments of field recordings), PCA, clusters, map coordinates, usage and cues. Relaunching loads them straight from disk; only new or changed files are processed, in batches, so an interrupted index resumes. Moved or renamed files are matched by a content fingerprint and keep their embedding, usage and cues.

## Build and run

Needs macOS 14+ and the Swift toolchain (Command Line Tools are enough; Xcode is not required).

```sh
scripts/bundle.sh          # builds build/ClapStudio.app (ad-hoc signed, not sandboxed)
open build/ClapStudio.app
scripts/test.sh            # unit + end-to-end tests
swift run -c release ClapBench 50000 2048   # scale benchmark
```

`scripts/snapshot.sh [out.png] [--select] [--map]` renders the app's window, toolbar included, to a PNG without screen capture permission or the window being on the current Space (debug builds only; see `DebugSnapshot.swift`). Handy for checking layout changes.

`scripts/env.sh` builds against the macOS 26 SDK when Xcode is absent, because the default macOS 27 SDK needs a SwiftUI macro plugin that only ships with Xcode. For a quick demo library, run `build/ClapStudio.app/Contents/MacOS/ClapStudio --add-folder /path/to/samples`.

## Similarity model

Three embedders sit behind one protocol:

1. **LAION-CLAP on Core ML** (preferred; HTSAT audio tower, 512-d, trained on audio/text pairs). Create it once with `tools/export_clap.py` (the docstring has the `uv` commands). It also exports the text tower and tokenizer files, which makes the one search box semantic: type "punchy 808 kick" or "warm analog pad" and you get files with that name first, then the sounds that best match the description.
2. **PANNs CNN14 on Core ML** (AudioSet classifier trunk, 2048-d). Create it with `tools/export_model.py`. Used when CLAP is absent.
3. **Built-in DSP features** (log-mel band statistics, envelope, centroid). Used automatically when no model is exported; the status bar says so. Good for coarse grouping, noticeably weaker than the neural models.

Models are compiled by the app on first launch. Switching model re-embeds the library once. `tools/make_references.py` regenerates the librosa / Hugging Face ground truth the tests compare the Swift front ends, tokenizer and Core ML outputs against.

Pipeline (CLAP: 10 s window at 48 kHz, repeat-padded; CNN14: 4 s at 32 kHz): standardise the sample, log-mel (vDSP), model, PCA to 128 dims, cosine search (BLAS), spherical k-means for colours, UMAP-style layout for the map.

Standardising (`SamplePreprocessor`, shared by indexing and query-by-example through `EmbeddingInput`): leading and trailing silence cut (loops keep theirs), onset at a fixed 5 ms pre-roll, one gain to -18 dBFS RMS with peaks at most -1 dBFS, quiet files boosted at most 30 dB. Loudness, length, tempo and pitch are measured on the original file, so the level and length aspects stay real; only the embedding sees the standardised sound. Changing this re-embeds the library.

At 50k samples x 2048 dims (M4 Pro): PCA 2.5 s, layout about 1 s, similarity query under 1 ms.

## Layout

- `Sources/ClapCore`: scanner, storage, audio, ML, indexing, PCA/k-means/layout, cues, playback (no UI).
- `Sources/ClapStudio`: SwiftUI app and Metal map.
- `Sources/ClapBench`: benchmark. `Tests/ClapCoreTests`: Swift Testing suites. `tools/`: model export.
